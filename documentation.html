1. Executive Summary & Field Background

1.1 Reservoir Context (Baghewala Field, Rajasthan)

The Baghewala Field in Rajasthan produces heavy crude oil (17.5° API) from the Jodhpur Sandstone formation at a setting depth of approximately 1,100 meters. The reservoir presents extreme operational challenges:

  • Ultra-High Crude Viscosity: 3,200 cP at natural reservoir temperature (47°C).
  • High Asphaltene Content: 14.5% asphaltene, causing precipitation risks and high viscous friction drag.
  • Low Primary Energy: Low reservoir temperature (46–48°C) and low reservoir pressure (35 bar).
  • Poor Primary Mobility: Requires thermal Enhanced Oil Recovery (EOR) via Cyclic Steam Stimulation (CSS) combined with Sucker Rod Pumping (SRP) artificial lift.

1.2 The Operational Problem: Unintegrated Optimization

Currently, CSS thermal injection cycles and SRP operational pumping speeds are optimized separately based on historical experience. As the reservoir cools following steam injection:

  1. Crude viscosity increases exponentially back toward baseline (3,200 cP).
  2. Viscous drag on the downstroke increases dramatically, overriding natural rod string gravity (Viscous Drag > Net Buoyant Weight).
  3. This results in Rod Floating, polished rod slack, rod string buckling, tubing wear, fluid pound, high Steam-Oil Ratio (SOR), excessive electrical power consumption, and frequent pump failures.

1.3 The Solution: Integrated Digital Twin

This software platform delivers an industrial physics-informed AI Digital Twin that continuously models and co-optimizes both CSS thermal injection cycles and SRP artificial lift speed profiles in real time, eliminating rod floating, reducing SOR, and maximizing net economic margin ($ NPV).

3. Mathematical Methodology & Physical Equations

3.1 Andrade Temperature-Viscosity Correlation

μ(T) = μref · exp[ B · ( 1 / (T + 273.15) - 1 / (Tref + 273.15) ) ]

Where:
- μref = 3,200 cP at Tref = 47°C (Ambient Baghewala Reservoir)
- B = 4,650 K (Activation energy coefficient for Jodhpur crude)
- At peak steam temperature (295°C), viscosity drops to 18 cP.

3.2 Thermal Reservoir Heat Decay Model T(t)

T(t) = Tres + (Tpeak - Tres) · exp[ - (λ / √(Vsteam / 2000)) · t ]

Where:
- Tres = 47°C, Tpeak = 295°C - (Soak Days × 3.5)
- λ = 0.045 day-1 (Thermal conductivity decay constant)

3.3 SRP Polished Rod Loads & Rod Floating Criterion

Wrod,net = Wrod,air · (1 - ρfluid / ρsteel)
Upstroke Load: Fup = Wrod,net + Ffluid + Faccel + Fviscous,up
Downstroke Load: Fdown = Wrod,net - Faccel - Fviscous,down

Rod Floating Condition:
When Viscous Drag Fviscous,down ∝ μ(T) · vdown exceeds net buoyant gravity Wrod,net, downstroke tension drops to 0 lbs, causing rod floating, slack line, and severe buckling.
